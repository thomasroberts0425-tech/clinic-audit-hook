# Voice

The skill produces two things:

1. **The observation** — one or two sentences that fill the `[PERSONALIZED OBSERVATION]`
   slot in the existing cold email template.
2. **A full email** built around that observation, first person, from Thomas.

Both come from the same finding. The observation is the load-bearing part; the email is
scaffolding around it.

## The register: customer-curious, not consultant-declarative

This is the whole thing. Every rule below follows from it.

You are not an auditor presenting findings. You are a person who went looking for
something on their website and couldn't find it, asking about it. From the field notes:

> "I went to your location, but your site didn't have this service — have you considered
> including this?"

> "My friend just went, had a great experience, went on your website but there's no
> testimonials on there to showcase this."

Both **describe an experience and ask a question.** Neither diagnoses. The stated
principle is *show, don't tell — diagnose, don't pitch*, and *bring the guard down first*.

A clinic owner reading a diagnosis gets defensive. A clinic owner reading "I went looking
for X and couldn't find it" gets curious, because that's a lost patient.

## Rules

- **Lead with the experience, not the finding.** "I went looking for your acupuncture
  page and couldn't find one" — not "your services are consolidated on a single page."
- **Ask, don't assert.** End the observation on a genuine question where you can. It
  invites a reply instead of a rebuttal.
- **No consultant vocabulary.** Ban: "gap", "structural", "opportunity", "leverage",
  "optimize", "conversion funnel", "digital presence", "leaving money on the table".
- **No credentials in the opener.** Rapport first. If a credibility line is needed it
  goes later in the email, and see the honesty rule below.
- **One observation.** Not three. Specificity is the credibility.
- **Vary the opening.** Do not start every email the same way. If the last five all
  opened "I was looking for..." then the skeleton has become the tell, and the whole
  point of personalising is lost. Rotate the entry point: the search that failed, the
  thing a patient asked about, the page that didn't exist, the comparison to a competitor
  who does have it.
- **Under 150 words. One CTA.**
- **Never state a number you did not measure.** No load times, no traffic estimates, no
  revenue figures.
- **Never invent a track record.** Do not claim clients, results, or years of experience.
  If a credibility line is wanted, leave `[credibility line]` for Thomas to fill. A
  fabricated reference is unrecoverable when they ask a follow-up question.

## Worked example

Healing Touch, Markham. Signals: online booking present, all four services on one
`/services` page, no address on the homepage.

**Observation** (fills the template bracket):

> I was trying to find your acupuncture page and couldn't — looks like everything's on
> the one services page. Is that deliberate?

**Full email:**

> Subject: acupuncture page on your site
>
> Hi — I was looking around for a clinic in Markham that does acupuncture and ended up
> on your site. Booking was easy to find, but when I went looking for acupuncture
> specifically there wasn't a page for it, just the one services page covering all four.
>
> Is that on purpose? Asking because it's likely why you don't come up for "acupuncture
> Markham" — Google needs a page to point at, and clinics that have one tend to pick up
> that search by default.
>
> I build websites for clinics. [credibility line] Happy to show you what I mean on a
> quick call if it's useful.
>
> — Thomas

Works because: it opens as a patient's experience, the finding arrives as a question,
the explanation is one plain sentence, and the ask is small. No diagnosis, no jargon,
no invented numbers.

## Bad

> Hi there — I noticed your website could use some improvements! Your site is slow, the
> design is outdated, and your mobile experience needs work. We help clinics modernize
> their digital presence.

Generic opener, three unevidenced findings, "slow" without measurement, no specific ask.

> Hi — your service pages are consolidated into a single URL, which is structurally
> limiting your organic search visibility and costing you an estimated $4,200/month in
> missed patient revenue.

Worse. Consultant vocabulary, and the figure is invented — one fabricated number destroys
everything true around it.

## When nothing fires

Say so and recommend skipping.

> No hook here. Booking works, per-service pages exist, NAP is complete, bios are live,
> site is responsive. Nothing on this website is worth opening a conversation about.
> Recommend skipping unless there's a non-website angle.

A skipped lead costs nothing. A bad first touch burns the only first impression that
clinic will ever give you.
